The research question

What can a public writing experiment tell us about AI-assisted task performance, and how should that evidence enter a business scenario?

AIWorkValue independently reanalyzes public data from Shakked Noy and Whitney Zhang’s 2023 experiment. The original experiment belongs to its authors. This project reconstructs the retained sample, organizes the data, checks estimates, and builds a transparent scenario model.

From experiment to analytical workflow

  1. Reconstruct the retained sample. Retain 453 participants and document available cases for each primary outcome.
  2. Build a validated warehouse. Use SQL/SQLite with explicit table grain, unique IDs, dimension and outcome checks, foreign keys, and round-trip validation.
  3. Estimate adjusted contrasts. Fit baseline-adjusted ANCOVA with occupation × randomized task-order and incentive-arm controls, HC3 robust intervals, and Holm adjustment across the two primary tests.
  4. Translate evidence into scenarios. Provide a Streamlit interface for exploring task volume, review effort, and costs under stated assumptions.

These are baseline-adjusted AI-offer contrasts in retained available cases. The estimates reproduce the rounded values in the committed analysis output. They describe the study’s short writing tasks and do not establish general workplace productivity gains.

My project contribution

The project combines data reconstruction, a validated SQL warehouse, reproducible statistical checks, SQL analysis, and an interactive workflow-value model. It brings the research evidence and the business assumptions into the same analytical workflow.

The business extension estimates hypothetical capacity value. Review effort, task frequency, labor cost, and other scenario inputs remain assumptions; they are not observed company savings.

Limits of the evidence

Available-case selection and differential attrition can affect interpretation. The source experiment concerns short professional writing tasks in early 2023. Rated quality does not measure factual accuracy, and exploratory baseline-score interactions do not establish general employee-ability effects.

Takeaway: a scenario model is most useful when readers can inspect both the empirical estimate and the assumptions required to apply it.
