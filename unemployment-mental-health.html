The research question

How are unemployment rates associated with mental health outcomes across U.S. states?

This University of Washington ECON 483 course project examines that relationship using state-level economic and health data. It was completed in Spring 2026 under course instructor Dong-Jae Eun.

Data and analytical approach

  1. Integrate public data. Combine CDC mental health indicators with BLS unemployment data at the state level.
  2. Account for observed differences. Include socioeconomic controls such as income and education in regression analysis.
  3. Examine interpretation. Discuss empirical estimates, statistical significance, and research limitations using R and Stata.

The completed work includes a research paper and final presentation. The project contributes experience in integrating public datasets, specifying regression models, and communicating empirical results with appropriate qualifications.

Interpretation and scope

A state-level association does not by itself identify an individual-level or causal effect. Interpretation must consider omitted factors, reverse causality, measurement differences, and aggregation.

The research record confirms the data sources, methods, and completed outputs. The paper and code are not publicly hosted here, so this case study does not report coefficients, effect sizes, or significance claims.

Takeaway: empirical research requires a clear distinction between the relationship a model estimates and the causal question a reader may want it to answer.
