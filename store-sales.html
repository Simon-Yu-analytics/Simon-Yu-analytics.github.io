The research question

How much do known promotion schedules improve 16-day retail sales forecasts, and how do conventional models compare with a pretrained time-series model?

The project uses the Kaggle Store Sales dataset from Corporación Favorita. It examines both competition accuracy and errors in sales units, which can lead to different model preferences.

Data and project context

3,000,888Training observations
1,782Store–family time series
16 daysCompetition forecast horizon

The data cover 54 stores and 33 product families. The competition test contains 28,512 rows for August 16–31, 2017. This project was developed for University of Washington ECON 481 in Autumn 2026.

Modeling and evaluation

  1. Establish meaningful benchmarks. Compare seasonal baselines, independently fitted Prophet models, pooled histogram gradient boosting, and pretrained t0-beta.
  2. Respect the forecast origin. Use chronological development and evaluation windows, check feature availability, and distinguish historical promotions from scheduled future promotions.
  3. Refine the ensemble. Add calendar features and product-family LightGBM models. V3 blends 50% calendar HGB with 700 boosting iterations, 25% family LightGBM, and 25% promotion-aware t0-beta on the log1p sales scale.
  4. Check the meaning of the metric. Compare RMSLE with daily MAE and WAPE rather than assuming the competition metric answers every operational question.

The recorded Kaggle RMSLE decreased from 0.47212 in V1 to 0.42172 in V3, a 10.68% relative reduction. The calculation is (0.47212 − 0.42172) ÷ 0.47212 × 100.

In the main historical promotion study, the pooled tree ranked first by RMSLE, while t0-beta had lower daily MAE and WAPE in every evaluation window. This illustrates why the evaluation metric should follow the intended decision.

Score provenance: the public V3 manifest records a student-provided Kaggle screenshot. The score is reported as documented in the repository; no independently inspected public leaderboard or rank is claimed.

Limitations and what I learned

These are historical results for one retailer. Later ensemble backtests reuse previously examined dates and should be read as retrospective comparisons. Promotion inputs assume the recorded future schedules were available at each cutoff.

Forecast accuracy alone does not establish causal promotion effects, reduced stockouts, inventory savings, or realized profit. A useful next step would be to evaluate a fixed model on a fresh period and connect forecast errors to explicit inventory costs.

Takeaway: a better RMSLE is a meaningful modeling result, but its operational value depends on how errors are weighted and how forecasts enter a decision.
